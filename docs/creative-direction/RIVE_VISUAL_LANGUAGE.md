# Rive Visual Language

Authority: Product Office package `RW-GOV-06`, extending `GOV-017` without replacing it.

## 1. Purpose

Rive Webworks has one recognisable visual language, multiple expressive registers and no generic page template. The governing instruction is:

> Standardise the grammar. Do not standardise the sentences.

Shared systems create coherence. Page-specific art direction creates character. Every future major page must use the grammar below to author a composition appropriate to its own subject, not assemble fragments from accepted pages.

## 2. Evidence base

The mature visual language is evidenced collectively by three accepted pages:

- **Industries** — immersive environmental storytelling, contextual photography, editorial scale and chapter contrast.
- **Work** — systemic explanation, evidence-led information architecture, controlled density and tonal modulation.
- **Connect** — intimate restraint, conversational typography, open whitespace and an integrated form with a deliberate no-media strategy.

Platforms supplies supporting systemic evidence through architecture and technical illustration. Evidence pages are quality references, not reusable layouts.

## 3. Relationship to the Rive Authored Page Standard

`GOV-017` and `docs/creative-direction/VISUAL_LANGUAGE.md` answer how a major page must be authored: visual thesis, narrative rhythm, media audit, memorable moments, responsive authorship and Human Design Review.

This document answers what mature Rive visual grammar that independent authorship operates within. It does not weaken any `GOV-017` gate. The permanent interpretation remains:

> Match Industries in thought, not in appearance.

## 4. Rive visual thesis

Rive is editorial, technically credible, warm and deliberate. It can become immersive when context matters, systemic when complexity must be understood and intimate when the experience should become human and direct.

The visual language favours meaning-bearing composition over interface chrome. Type, media, rules, surfaces, space and interaction each require a narrative job. A clean, consistent page is the baseline; a major page also needs an identifiable idea and at least one memorable, subject-specific moment.

## 5. Shared visual grammar

### Global invariants

- the governed serif/sans type system and canonical semantic scale;
- the established colour tokens, spacing/container system and accessible interaction language;
- the global header, navigation and footer architecture;
- keyboard, focus, contrast, reduced-motion, responsive and dark-mode quality;
- claims safety and editorial restraint.

### Strong defaults

- serif for conceptual emphasis and substantial ideas, sans for explanation and operation;
- warm neutral, white and navy surfaces orchestrated according to narrative state;
- burgundy as micro-detail and gold as a scarce emphasis or action colour;
- fine rules, alignment and spacing before unnecessary containers;
- controlled asymmetry where it clarifies hierarchy;
- media selected for meaning rather than quota.

### Available devices

- environmental or human photography;
- contextual illustration and technical diagrams;
- dark chapters and tonal resets;
- indices and numbering;
- immersive background imagery;
- oversized conceptual typography;
- evidence surfaces, progressive disclosure and restrained interaction;
- large statement/action conclusions.

Available devices are never mandatory. Their success on one page does not make them global components or defaults.

### Page-specific expressions

Industries' Field Index, Healthcare environment and CREDIBILITY / SCALE field; Work's layered-system and evidence compositions; and Connect's three-question scaffold and open working-note form remain page-specific. Do not extract them into universal page recipes.

## 6. Three visual registers

Registers are creative modes, not templates.

### Immersive register

Primary evidence: Industries.

Use when environment, people, physical context, consequence, place, scale or atmosphere materially advances the story. Likely tools include contextual photography, substantial inline media, rare background imagery, spatial transitions and editorial type.

Do not turn this register into stock-photo decoration, one-image-per-section repetition, generic agency photography or habitual full bleed.

### Systemic register

Primary evidence: Work. Supporting evidence: Platforms.

Use when the visitor must understand architecture, relationships, evidence, process, technical depth, layered capability or accountability. Likely tools include diagrams, rules, evidence surfaces, structured composition, restrained data visualisation, progressive disclosure and light/dark modulation.

Do not turn this register into dashboard theatre, SaaS feature grids, decorative flowcharts, excessive cards or fake complexity.

### Intimate register

Primary evidence: Connect.

Use when trust, conversation, focus, reflection, low cognitive load or direct interaction is central. Likely tools include typography, whitespace, fine rules, sparse colour, conversational prompts, open forms and restrained composition.

Do not turn this register into empty minimalism, unfinished layouts, centred contact cards, chat-interface mimicry or arbitrary whitespace.

## 7. Register combination

A page may use a primary register and a secondary register when the narrative genuinely changes mode. A Design Decision Brief must state:

1. dominant register;
2. secondary register, if any;
3. transition point;
4. reason for the transition;
5. narrative purpose served.

Do not mechanically assign a register or combine one device from each evidence page. Register selection does not authorize a predefined layout.

## 8. Typography

Serif generally carries page theses, major ideas, emotional statements, conceptual moments and substantial editorial emphasis. Sans generally carries explanation, labels, metadata, navigation, operational information and interface text. This is a relationship, not a mechanical heading/body rule.

`/work/` remains the canonical reference for equivalent role sizes. Distinguish hero H1, editorial H2, section H2, H3, lead, body, support, eyebrow, metadata, CTA and conceptual display roles. An authored departure must remain semantic, local and documented; do not create a new global scale to reproduce a screenshot.

Typography may become visual material when the words themselves carry the concept. Ask why the phrase deserves visual scale. It must preserve actual hierarchy and accessibility and must not become a decorative watermark or a copied CREDIBILITY / SCALE treatment.

## 9. Eyebrows and micro-typography

Small uppercase labels orient rather than decorate. Use considered casing, tracking, spacing and semantic placement. Numbering should clarify sequence, chapter or relationship. Burgundy is a strong default for this micro-register, but repeated labels must not become automatic visual garnish.

## 10. Colour hierarchy

- **Navy / near-navy:** authority, primary text and substantial dark chapters.
- **Warm cream / neutral:** warmth, continuity, atmosphere and page grounding.
- **White / near-white:** clarity, breathing room and tonal reset.
- **Burgundy:** micro-accent, eyebrow, numbering, rule and precise editorial detail.
- **Gold:** important action, selective conceptual emphasis and rare authored moments.
- **Mineral Azure:** an available technical/interactive accent where the existing token guidance supports it; it is not required by the three-register model.

Gold remains meaningful because it is scarce. Do not spread it across routine headings, borders and decoration. Dark mode requires authored token choices rather than brighter gold or mechanical inversion.

## 11. Tonal orchestration

Surface changes are narrative events. A page may move through warm neutral, white, navy, photography and restrained colour fields, but must not alternate backgrounds mechanically.

Every Section Rhythm Map records `Surface / tonal state` alongside narrative role, visual mode and energy. A surface transition should mark orientation, immersion, analysis, intimacy, evidence or conclusion—not merely create variety.

## 12. Whitespace

Whitespace is active design material. It may create hierarchy, pause, anticipation, separation, focus, breathing room or compositional tension. Review both compression and emptiness. Ask, “What is this space doing?” If there is no answer, investigate the composition; do not automatically fill it.

Connect is evidence that no media and substantial space can be fully authored. Industries and Work show that open space is equally important between high-density or media-rich chapters.

## 13. Composition

Rive supports controlled asymmetry, varied measures and unequal visual weights when they communicate hierarchy or direction. Centring, symmetry and equal columns remain valid when meaning requires them, not as defaults. Random imbalance is a defect.

Compose the relationship between content, media and space before selecting containers. Major storytelling sections should usually be meaning-specific enough to resist movement unchanged to another page.

## 14. Rules and micro-detail

Fine horizontal and vertical rules, restrained borders, numbering and local accent lines can organise information more elegantly than boxes. They should clarify alignment, relationship, sequence or boundary. If removing a rule changes neither comprehension nor composition, reconsider it. Do not turn every section into editorial line art.

## 15. Anti-card guidance

Cards are appropriate when content represents genuinely discrete, independently actionable or comparable objects. They are not the default organisational primitive. Before introducing a grid, ask whether spacing, alignment, type, rules, imagery, surfaces or sequencing can communicate the structure more directly.

Anti-card does not mean card prohibition. Semantically correct containment should not be sacrificed to perform editorialism.

## 16. Media philosophy

For every substantial section classify media as `REQUIRED`, `BENEFICIAL`, `OPTIONAL` or `NO MEDIA`, with a reason. Use media when it materially improves storytelling, comprehension, atmosphere or visual quality. No-media decisions are valid and authored; Connect is the clearest evidence.

Every asset must answer: what does this communicate that surrounding text and structure cannot communicate as effectively?

## 17. Photography

Use photography for environment, humanity, organisational reality, atmosphere, consequence, place, scale or contextual recognition. Prefer believable editorial imagery with deliberate cropping and responsive behavior.

Reject generic teams, laptop meetings, handshakes, boardrooms, random server racks, blue-lit “technology”, obvious AI artefacts and filler. Do not infer clients, facilities, projects or proof from contextual imagery.

## 18. Illustration and diagrams

Use illustration when abstraction communicates better than photography. Use diagrams when relationships need explanation. Suitable subjects include architecture, layers, infrastructure, workflow, dependency, evidence, systems and transformation.

Illustrations and diagrams must be specific, restrained, statically understandable, responsive and accessible through equivalent text. Never add them merely to make a section appear technical.

## 19. Background imagery

Zero to two substantial background-image treatments per page is the normal ceiling, not a target. Every use documents narrative purpose, why background is superior to inline media, focal point, text-safe zone, desktop/tablet/mobile crop, dark-mode treatment and accessibility treatment.

Industries Healthcare is evidence of a strong environmental chapter, not a reusable section. If legibility requires a heavy rescue overlay, use a better asset or inline treatment.

## 20. Section rhythm

A major page should behave like chapters whose energies may be quiet, orienting, explanatory, immersive, analytical, intimate or decisive. Repeated split layouts, equal columns, card grids, centred headings, diagrams, background images and CTA bands must be challenged when consecutive.

Variation must follow narrative purpose. Random alternation is not authorship. The map must record narrative role, visual mode, surface, energy, media role and transitions to adjacent sections.

## 21. Memorable moments

Major pages identify approximately one or two memorable moments derived from their story. Industries uses an immersive Healthcare environment and meaning-bearing editorial type. Work uses layered systems and evidence compositions. Connect uses a conversational question scaffold and open form surface.

Their lack of a shared technique is the lesson. A memorable moment is defined by narrative impact, not spectacle. One strong moment is preferable to several weak effects.

## 22. CTA and conclusion behavior

A conversion section exists when the narrative needs a conclusion. Strong Rive conclusions often use a large statement, clear action, breathing room and an asymmetric relationship with little competing content. Work and Industries demonstrate this behavior without establishing a universal component.

Connect demonstrates that a page may conclude in its primary interaction and need no additional pre-footer CTA. Ask what action naturally follows this page's story; stable destinations do not require identical presentation.

## 23. Mobile authorship

At approximately 375px, define what remains of the thesis. Explicitly author section and media order, crop, display-type reduction, diagram simplification, whitespace compression, rule behavior, hierarchy, CTA width and memorable-moment transformation. Mobile is not desktop stacked vertically.

The register must remain legible: immersive context survives through intentional crops and cadence; systemic relationships simplify rather than miniaturise; intimate experiences preserve focus and generous touch interaction without arbitrary vertical emptiness.

## 24. Dark-mode authorship

Dark mode preserves identity and mood without mechanical inversion. Review image exposure, navy relationships, cream substitution, gold intensity, burgundy visibility, rules, diagram hierarchy, form surfaces and perceived whitespace. A mode may legitimately change atmosphere while retaining the same thesis.

## 25. Global versus local decisions

Classify every material design decision:

| Class | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Global | Required shared foundation | typefaces, semantic scale, colour tokens, grid/container, shell, accessibility, focus |
| Strong default | Preferred unless the page thesis gives a reason to depart | serif concepts, sans explanation, burgundy micro-detail, restrained gold, fine rules, controlled asymmetry |
| Available device | Approved but optional tool | dark chapter, background image, diagram, index, conceptual display type, substantial media |
| Page-specific | Expression owned by one narrative | Industries CREDIBILITY / SCALE, Work layered system, Connect question scaffold |

Local innovation must not become global merely because it succeeded once. Missing tokens or component opportunities discovered during review are recommendations for a separate package.

## 26. Anti-template and anti-AI guidance

Challenge automatic centred heroes, repeated splits, card or Bento grids, rounded containers, gradients, glow, glass, pills, floating objects, fake dashboards, generic icon rows, identical padding, arbitrary huge words and decorative motion. None is universally banned; unreasoned use is.

At final review ask whether the page would still feel subject-specific without its logo, colours and copy. Brand styling alone is not authorship.

## 27. Cross-page differentiation and content-swap test

Compare each future major page against Industries, Work, Connect, Platforms and other relevant accepted pages. Record the closest page, similarity risk, conceptual and compositional differences, media difference, memorable-moment difference and the new contribution to Rive's visual vocabulary.

Ask whether another page's content could enter the composition with minimal structural change. Shared functional components may pass that test by design; major storytelling composition usually should not.

## 28. Full-page and squint tests

Review a complete desktop capture at full scale and reduced scale. At full scale, assess chapters, media purpose, whitespace, monotony and conclusion. At reduced scale, assess mass, colour, surface transitions, density, image distribution, pacing and major moments while copy is secondary.

If the page becomes a repetitive series of similar rectangles, investigate. Full-page and squint artifacts supplement, not replace, viewport-specific responsive review.

## 29. Evidence-page annotations

### Shared across all three

- expressive serif paired with operational sans;
- warm, white and navy relationships within the established palette;
- restrained burgundy detail and scarce gold;
- alignment, fine rules and whitespace carrying structure;
- controlled asymmetry and deliberate hierarchy;
- page-specific rhythm rather than repeated component order;
- authored responsive transformation and stable global shell.

### Industries uniquely contributes

Environmental image mass, immersive context, alternating media-rich/media-light chapters, typography as meaning-bearing media and a decisive outcome-oriented conclusion.

### Work uniquely contributes

Evidence-led structure, systematic explanation, multiple information modes, controlled density, technical relationships and a conclusion shaped around accountable work.

### Connect uniquely contributes

Intimacy, human invitation, meaningful no-media restraint, conversational prompts, open form integration and a page whose core interaction is its conclusion.

### What must never be unified

Their silhouettes, section sequences, media counts, memorable devices, density, CTA behavior and emotional register. Do not retrofit shared components merely to increase visible similarity.

### Later inconsistencies to evaluate separately

Work still contains pending media placeholders, and some older accepted pages predate the mature authored-page standard. These facts do not authorize changes in this package. Any refinement or token work requires a separate Product Office commission.

## 30. Future-page commissioning checklist

Before JSX implementation, confirm:

- [ ] distinct visual thesis and storytelling metaphor;
- [ ] primary and optional secondary register with transition rationale;
- [ ] section rhythm including surface/tonal state;
- [ ] media classified as required, beneficial, optional or no media;
- [ ] approximately one or two subject-specific memorable moments;
- [ ] typography, whitespace, rule and colour strategy;
- [ ] selective gold and purposeful burgundy;
- [ ] closest-page and new-contribution declaration;
- [ ] template and content-swap resistance plan;
- [ ] authored mobile and dark-mode transformations;
- [ ] full-page, squint and Human Design Review artifacts planned;
- [ ] Product Office visual acceptance remains the final gate.

The target is equivalent authorship, not equivalent spectacle: understand the intelligence demonstrated by Industries, Work and Connect, preserve Rive's shared grammar and author a new composition appropriate to each page's meaning.
